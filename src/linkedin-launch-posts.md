# LinkedIn launch sequence — OH SH!T, Part Two

Three posts in a deliberate order over roughly ten days. Post one goes with the 7-slide carousel you already have. Posts two and three build on different angles so you're not just repeating the announcement.

**Rules of thumb that matter on LinkedIn right now:**
- The first 2–3 lines are everything — they decide whether anyone hits "see more".
- Don't put external links in the post body. LinkedIn throttles them. Put links in the first comment.
- Tag people and organisations that might reshare: Bowel Cancer UK, GRC Solutions, IT Governance, Katrina Macdermid, Kevin Donaghy, Sophie (if she's on LinkedIn).
- Post mid-morning UK time (Tuesday–Thursday is strongest).
- Reply to every comment in the first two hours. LinkedIn reads that as signal.

## Post 2 — The AI angle (48 hours after Post 1)
**Pair with:** a single screenshot of your symptom-tracker output, or a quote card with one of the three surprises.
**Publish:** ~2 days after Post 1. This is the one that might travel outside your immediate circle.

---

Halfway through chemotherapy, I started using AI to track my symptoms.

Not to be clever. I was too tired and too foggy to hold it all in my head — which side effects had started when, what was new, what was fading, what I’d already told my oncology team and what I’d forgotten to.

So I started dumping it all into Claude at the end of each day.

Three things surprised me.

**1. It spotted patterns I’d missed.** "Your fizzy hands get worse on day 3 after infusion." A brain in the chemo fog genuinely can’t assemble that.

**2. It gave me back some control.** Instead of arriving at oncology fumbling for the right words, I could show up with a one-page summary that actually helped the team help me.

**3. It worked for a fellow patient too.** We compared notes, refined the prompts, and — fair warning — we’ve written it up so anyone going through this can use it.

The full method is in Part Two of the diaries (out now — link in the comments). Short version: treat AI as a dumb-but-tireless memory. Not a doctor. A witness.

If AI in healthcare feels like hype to you — fair. A lot of it is. But sometimes the thing you actually need isn’t diagnosis or decision-support. It’s just something that remembers.

## After launch week — cadence
Keep going at roughly once a week. Rotate four content pillars so you never get stuck:

1. **Extracts from the diary** — one per week. Paste the raw text, end with "from Part One / Part Two — link in comments." The book does its own marketing when the words are good.
2. **Advocacy** — screening stats, the "1 in 17" clip, what symptoms not to ignore, Bowel Cancer UK campaigns.
3. **The writing / survivor process** — what you learned, what you wish you’d known, what surprised you.
4. **Reader responses** — every review, every message you get ("this helped my dad"). With permission, post them. Social proof compounds.

## Amplification — do once, benefits forever
- DM the foreword authors with the three posts above; ask if they’d be willing to reshare one.
- Offer 15–20 free Part Two copies to named healthcare / GRC / publishing voices on LinkedIn in exchange for a three-sentence public reaction if they like it. Three will bite. That’s three endorsements from audiences adjacent to yours.
- Pitch three podcasts this month: one health/memoir, one GRC-adjacent for the AI angle, one local UK (Surrey / Sussex press or podcast) for the hometown hook.
