# Testimonial request email — send to 5 past clients today

Three will reply. Two of those will give you a usable quote. That's enough to transform the homepage social proof strip from "placeholder" to "credible".

**Who to send to:**
Pick five past consulting / mentoring / speaking clients where the engagement went well. If you're unsure, start with the ones who've stayed in touch — those are the easy wins.

**Tone:** warm, specific, low-friction. The easier you make it to reply, the higher the response rate.

---

## The email

**Subject line (pick one):**

```
A small ask — would you vouch for me?
```

```
Quick favour — and completely fine to say no
```

```
A two-minute favour, if you're up for it
```

---

**Body:**

```
Hi [Name],

Hope you're well.

I'm refreshing the Sol Seven Studio website this month — properly, for the first time in a while — and I'd love to include a short quote from you on the homepage if you're happy to be there.

Nothing formal needed. Two or three sentences about our work together — what we did, what changed, and whether you'd work with me again. Your name, role and organisation alongside it (or anonymised if you'd prefer — "CIO, UK financial services" works fine).

Absolutely no pressure if now isn't the right time, or if company policy makes it tricky. Just thought I'd ask.

Here are three prompts in case they help — pick any one:

1. What was the before/after of our work together?
2. What surprised you most about how we approached it?
3. Would you recommend me to a peer, and if so, how would you describe me to them?

Any length is fine. A single sentence is fine. Completely fine if the answer is "not right now" too.

Thanks [Name] — either way, great to be in touch.

[Your sign-off]
```

---

## Follow-up (one week later, only if no reply)

Keep this *really* short:

```
Hi [Name] — gentle nudge on the below, absolutely no obligation. If now's not the moment, no worries at all and I'll stop bothering you.

[Forwarded original email]

[Your sign-off]
```

Don't send a third chase. If they don't reply to the nudge, leave it.

---

## Once you have 3 quotes back

Drop them into the homepage social proof strip in this format:

```
"Short, specific quote about a concrete outcome."
— Name, Role, Organisation
```

Pro tip: if one of the quotes is outstanding, **don't** bury it alongside two average ones. Put the best one in a dedicated "featured testimonial" block just under the hero, and the other two in a three-up strip further down. One great quote above the fold is worth more than three average ones in a row.

---

## If the quote needs a small polish

It's completely fair to lightly edit a quote — trim filler, fix grammar, tighten length — as long as the meaning is unchanged. **Always send the edited version back to the person for approval before it goes live.** Two lines: "Here's how I've lightly trimmed this for the site — happy with this wording? Let me know if you'd like any changes." Done.

Never change the meaning. Never attribute something they didn't say.
