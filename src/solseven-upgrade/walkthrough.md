## 1. Hero — add a primary CTA button (10 minutes)

**Where in Wix:** Home page → click on the hero area → Add Element → Button.

Right now your hero has a headline and subhead but **no button**. That's the biggest remaining issue up top — visitors have nowhere obvious to click. Add one button directly under your new subhead copy.

**Button text (pick one):**

```
Book a 20-minute intro call
```

```
Work with me
```

```
Let's talk
```

I'd go with the first — it's specific, low-commitment, and sets expectations.

**Link the button to:** your Wix Bookings page, specifically the "Value-Led Service Management Consultancy" service (the "A conversation about 'What value means to you'" one — that's already framed as an intro conversation, perfect).

**Style:** solid fill, dark background, white text. Match the Sol Seven logo tones if you can — either the black or the terracotta/salmon from the logo.

**Optional: tweak the hero headline.**

Your current headline reads "Consultancy, Mentoring, Speaking, Writing" — a list. It works, but it doesn't sell anything. If you want to sharpen it, try:

```
Making IT mean something.
```

Short, confident, and leaves the visitor curious. Pair it with your new subhead and it sings.

## 2. About David section — lead with credentials, not origin story (20 minutes)

**Where in Wix:** Home page → scroll to the "David Barrow CITP FBCS" text block → edit text.

**The problem:** your current paragraph buries the headline. It's a run-on sentence that mentions ITIL, BCS, BSI, conferences, and chairmanship all at the same weight. A visitor skims it and remembers nothing.

**Replace it with this structure — a one-liner hook, then three short "credential bullets", then a closing line:**

```
David Barrow, CITP FBCS — Co-founder & Service Management expert

30 years in enterprise IT. Three decades of making service management a source of value, not a cost centre.

→ ITIL 4 Master & ITIL Ambassador
→ Chartered IT Professional and Fellow of the BCS
→ Chairperson, BSI IT Service Management Committee — the UK's leading voice on the ISO committee
→ International speaker at itSMFUK, SDI and SRVision conferences

David's mission is to make service management a more accessible career that adds value — to organisations, to teams, and to the world.
```

**Why it works:**
- The "30 years" hook is concrete and fast to absorb
- The bulleted credentials are scannable — most visitors will skim
- The closing line ties back to "value-led" from the hero, so the whole page rhymes

**Small typo in your current copy:** "Davids passions is to make service management…" → the apostrophe and plural are off. The rewrite above fixes it.

## 3. Social proof strip — this is the highest-leverage addition (45 minutes)

**Where in Wix:** Add a new Strip section directly beneath the hero (before "About David").

**Why it matters:** you have A-tier credentials (BSI chair, ITIL Ambassador, itSMFUK/SDI/SRVision speaker) and zero social proof on the homepage. That's free engagement left on the table. A logo bar + one or two testimonials will do more for conversion than almost anything else.

**Build it in two rows:**

**Row 1 — "As seen at" logo bar:**

Heading:

```
Trusted by the UK's service management community
```

Logos to source (these are all public; grab them from the orgs' press kits or their own sites):
- itSMFUK
- SDI (Service Desk Institute)
- SRVision
- BSI (British Standards Institution)
- BCS (The Chartered Institute for IT)

Aim for 5 logos in a single row, greyscale, equal height. Wix has a "Logo strip" element that handles this.

**Row 2 — testimonials (3 slots, placeholder text until real ones come back):**

Use Wix's Testimonial element (or a 3-column repeater with a Strip). Placeholder text structure:

```
"Short, specific quote about a concrete outcome."
— Name, Role, Organisation
```

See `testimonial-email.md` in this folder for the email to send to five past clients **today**. Three will come back with quotes within a week. Use them to replace the placeholders.

Until the real quotes land, you can seed with 1–2 **public pull-quotes** if you have any — e.g. if an itSMFUK organiser or conference attendee tweeted something nice about one of your talks, with their permission quote it.

## 4. Services section — add price anchors (15 minutes)

**Where in Wix:** Home page → Wix Bookings widget → edit each service description.

Currently all three services are "Request to Book" / "Price on Application". That's friction. Add a price anchor to the description so people self-select:

**Value-Led Service Management Consultancy:**

Current: "Listening, empathising and empowering you to overcome the challenges you face."

Replace with:

```
One-to-one mentoring for service management professionals at any career stage. 6-session packages from £XXX, or single sessions available. Drop me a note to find the right fit.
```

**Public Speaking:**

Current: "Introduce your digital teams to value co-creation"

Replace with:

**Two options, in order of effort:**

**Option A (easy):** Replace with a professional headshot of you speaking at itSMFUK, SDI, or SRVision — David-at-the-mic. If you have one with an audience visible in the background, even better. It instantly positions you as "international conference speaker" rather than "ceramics studio."

## 10. Mixed messaging — the audience question (decision, not a task)

Your nav currently mixes professional consulting (Service Management Consultancy, Mentoring, Public Speaking) with creative / personal (Ceramics, Sol Seven Team). A CTO hiring a consultant sees "Ceramics" and wonders what they're buying.

**Decide between two paths:**

**Path 1 — keep both, separate them.** Change the nav to two groups:
- Work with me: Consultancy / Mentoring / Speaking / Writing
- The Studio: Ceramics / Team / Blog

**Path 2 — spin ceramics out.** Move ceramics to a standalone Instagram grid or mini-site, link to it from the About page only. Solsevenstudio.com becomes a pure professional consultancy presence.

No rush on this one — but worth deciding before you go hard on LinkedIn marketing, so traffic lands on a page that's unambiguous about what you do.
